Interpill – Example testing scenarios

Optional prompts to help you explore the app during testing.

You may use Interpill in any way that feels natural to you. The scenarios below are designed to help you try different parts of the app. You do not need to complete all of them.

Scenario 1 – Checking two medicines together

  1. Find two medicines in the app (for example Caffeine and Ibuprofen).
  2. Run an interaction check for them.
  3. Read the result and any highlighted warnings.
  4. Decide whether the explanation feels clear.

Scenario 2 – Checking a medicine that may not be suitable

  1. Imagine that the person checking the medicine has a history of stomach ulcers.
  2. Look up Aspirin and check how the app presents any risks.
  3. Review the warnings and consider how visible they are.

Scenario 3 – Allergy-related risk

  1. Imagine a person with an allergy to fish or fish oil.
  2. Find a product containing Omega-3 acids and check its information.
  3. See whether the app highlights anything relevant to the allergy.

Scenario 4 – Trying the daily medication schedule

  1. Add two medicines of your choice to a daily schedule (for example one in the morning, one in the evening).
  2. Check how the schedule displays what is taken on that day.
  3. Use the quick interaction check from the schedule if it is available.

Scenario 5 – Looking up detailed information on one medicine

  1. Search for a medicine you know (for example Paracetamol).
  2. Open its information page and scroll through warnings, interactions and other sections.
  3. See whether the layout helps you find the information you expect.

Scenario 6 – Scanning a real medicine package (real device only)

(This scenario is not available in the browser-based emulator.)

  1. Use the scanning feature to scan a medicine package (for example Paracetamol or Ibuprofen).
  2. Confirm or correct the recognised medicine name.
  3. Explore the information or run an interaction check if you wish.

Please remember that Interpill is a research prototype and not a substitute for professional medical advice. Do not change any real treatment based on this app.
